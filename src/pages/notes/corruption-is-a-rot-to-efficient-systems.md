I am not (at present) a socialist. But I'm a big sympathizer for the problems (self proclaimed) socialists point out. Most systems are like this. Their critique bears more truth than their solutions. 

It's not hard to feel that something is wrong or unpleasant. You can publicize these feelings in a way that is difficult for anyone to deny. Solutions are complicated. Most things aren't obvious. It's likely you're missing a few things. 

When I hear critiques about systems such that Socialism is a proposed solution, it's usually to do with equity. Some system isn't fair. 

The golden example is usually how capitalism deals with wealth as itself an asset to distribute and generate wealth off of. I actually agree with this framing completely and won't talk much about it for the rest of the article.

Outside of this (i.e. that it's too hard when you're poor and too easy when you're rich), the complaints around capitalism tend to do with corruption. 

This topic is far more approachable for the average citizen. Someone is screwing you over by withholding information or power. It feels bad.

Just over the last week I watched videos on [parking apps hiding fees](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=i8mrwS5QZQE) with dark patterns and [airports overcharging for food](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=35ypZJ3Zc0Y).  These topics are especially interesting since there are governing bodies overseeing both. Elected officials are turning a blind eye to a bad deal for the citizens they were elected by.

This is corruption. 

Economists talk about [Pareto efficiency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pareto_efficiency) - an ideal state of an economic system where no one can be made better off without making someone else worse off. Lots of economic systems are just worse for both parties because they're not properly efficient. Corruption can be Pareto efficient only for its instance, but is not efficient for all the things it touches. 

That is to say, corruption is a rot to efficient systems.

Hank Green has some [good videos on this](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YVdQjqUXuDc). Even for the capitalist of capitalists, corruption is a rot. It perverts the goals of the society it serves. Not only is it unethical in some nominal sense of the word and our shared relative morality, but it makes systems worse. 

I'm talking in too many abstractions. Here are some examples.

A corrupt bank (one that loses your money) creates distrust in banking. People stop pooling their money and this the bank has less capital to loan out to large construction. Money is stored elsewhere, away from the community. The one corrupt banker enriches themself, but destroys a locale.

Gerrymandering is the process of redistricting such that the majority of votes do not necessarily dictate who is elected. Your desired party wins, but the governing bodies no longer need to care about everyone they govern over. People are forgotten and their needs are ignored.

A corrupt government will place family members in positions of power and favor loyalty over meritocracy. Scientists are not involved in decision making. Jobs are not created based on need. Work is not directed towards what improves the population's living conditions. 

There are many ways capitalist systems disproportionately distribute power. Corruption is agnostic to economic systems. That corruption exists is its own blight and not in the favor of capitalists at large. 

A billionaire may improve their surroundings through NIMBY policy, but overtime grows to be awed by the European settlements they vacation to - fully unaware that the ideal that seems so far away could be captured by simply allowing close construction, walkable cities, and public transport. Giving these people outsized power is its own corruption. It warps the systems to serve only the few at the expense of the many and ultimately back again at these same few. The few are not bettered for destroying the populace. 

I am only bringing this up in the context of capitalism and socialism because I occupy an annoying intersection of spaces such that I'm exposed to both camps. Corruption is good for no one. It also does not require upending your economic system to improve.

One can't be confident in any foolproof way to fix corruption wholesale (modern day and historical socialist leaning economies were rotted by it too), but there are strategies. The most obvious of the bunch is freedom of information. 

It seems somewhat clear that certain kinds of information propagation machines can be hazardous. Disinformation can cause riots. But on the whole, a better informed populace can vote more aligned people into power.
